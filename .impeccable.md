## Design Context

### Users
Businesses that use permitting software — permit expeditors, licensed contractors, architects, and real estate developers. These are professional, task-focused users who need fast, trustworthy answers before project submission or client calls. They work in offices and on job sites. They are not exploring; they have a specific question and need a credible answer quickly. Mobile usability is required — field use on phones is a real scenario.

### Brand Personality
**Authoritative, precise, civic.**

This is a serious professional tool. It should feel like the best-designed government information system that was never actually built — structured, trustworthy, and efficient, with a modern SaaS layer of polish. Not a startup. Not consumer-friendly. Not playful. The product speaks with the confidence of an expert, not the enthusiasm of a salesperson.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light mode. Business hours, professional context, office and field use.
- **Visual tone**: Tight, structured, information-dense without feeling cluttered. Clean typography with strong hierarchy. Minimal decoration — every visual element earns its place by aiding comprehension or navigation.
- **Anti-patterns to avoid**: Gradient text, glowing accents, hero metrics with big numbers, rounded card grids that look like a SaaS landing page, anything that reads "AI product demo." Also avoid startup playfulness — soft pastels, oversized icons, animated sparkles.
- **Reference feeling**: A well-maintained municipal planning portal crossed with a modern legal research tool — precise, scannable, unambiguous. Not pretty for its own sake; pretty because it's organized.
- **Color**: The existing slate-blue OKLCH palette is correct and should be maintained. Primary blue reserved strictly for CTAs, active states, and focus rings. Surfaces stay neutral. No accent overuse.
- **Typography**: Geist is the right call — civic-professional weight without the tech-monoculture of Inter. Body at 15px, labels at 13px, headings at 20px.
- **Mobile**: Layouts must adapt gracefully to single-column on mobile. Forms, results, and tables must be readable at 375px width without horizontal scroll.

### Design Principles
1. **Legibility before everything** — Permit code text is dense. The UI must never fight the content for attention. Generous line-height, constrained line lengths (65–72ch), clear typographic hierarchy.
2. **Professional density** — Business users are comfortable with information-rich interfaces. Show relevant metadata (page numbers, section IDs, zoning codes) without hiding it behind extra interactions.
3. **Trustworthy, not flashy** — Visual restraint signals reliability. If a design decision makes the UI look cooler but doesn't improve comprehension or workflow, cut it.
4. **Mobile as a peer, not an afterthought** — Field use is real. Forms and result panels must function fully at phone width, not just technically render.
5. **Civic tone in copy** — Sentence-case, no exclamation marks, no AI marketing language. The writing should feel like a knowledgeable colleague, not a product pitch.
